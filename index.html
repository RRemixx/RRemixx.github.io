About

I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Prof. Alexander Rodríguez in the Complexity AI Lab.

My research focuses on uncertainty quantification for time series forecasting: designing reliable prediction intervals for non-stationary, multi-step settings with conformal prediction, flow matching, and Bayesian inference. I also build agentic frameworks for infectious disease modeling. My work is applied to public health forecasting (CDC FluSight).

Research interests: conformal prediction · probabilistic forecasting · flow matching · Bayesian inference · LLM agents · epidemic scenario modeling

Publications

  1. Simulate, Reason, Decide: Scientific Reasoning with LLMs for Simulation-Driven Decision Making
    Yuhan Yang, Ruipu Li, Alexander Rodríguez
    SciSoc Agents & LLMs Workshop, KDD 2026 Oral · Under review at NeurIPS 2026
    arXiv

    MechSim describes scientific simulators in a structured schema and lets LLM agents reason over it, linking simulator outcomes to the mechanisms that caused them.

  2. Optimization-based Online Conformal Prediction for Multi-step Forecasting
    Ruipu Li, Daniel Menacho, Alexander Rodríguez
    Workshop on Uncertainty Reasoning and Quantification in Decision Making (UDM), KDD 2026 Oral · Under review at AAAI-27
    arXiv

    O2CP keeps per-horizon long-run coverage while jointly optimizing across horizons for sharper intervals; it beats its base method in 53 of 60 comparisons across five domains.

  3. Neural Conformal Control for Time Series Forecasting
    Ruipu Li, Alexander Rodríguez
    AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI), 2025, main track
    PaperarXivCode

    A neural controller for online conformal prediction that adapts intervals under distribution shift, with coverage guarantees and consistent quantiles.
